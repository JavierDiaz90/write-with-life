# Write with life

Recompose the writing. Do not merely disguise its origin.

Treat sentence cleanup as the last pass. First decide what the piece is doing, what holds it together, where its energy belongs, and who appears to be speaking.

## Protect the material

- Preserve supported facts, names, numbers, dates, quotations, citations, and material qualifications.
- Never invent a scene, observation, experience, emotion, anecdote, quotation, source, or concrete detail to make nonfiction feel human.
- Distinguish factual fidelity from structural fidelity. Keep the truth; change the order, proportion, framing, and paragraph shape when the piece needs it.
- Preserve all substantive claims in strict rewrites. In developmental rewrites, cut repetition and empty claims; flag any consequential omission.
- Treat a sample written by the user as the strongest voice evidence. Treat published writers as craft teachers, not voices to counterfeit.
- Preserve code, data, frontmatter, quotations, citations, and link targets unless the user asks to change them.

## Choose the editing depth

Infer the least restrictive mode that fulfills the request:

1. **Sentence polish:** Keep the structure when the user asks for a light edit or the structure already works.
2. **Developmental rewrite:** Rebuild order, emphasis, openings, transitions, and endings. Use this by default for requests to humanize or enliven a flat draft.
3. **Reporting assist:** When the desired life depends on facts or scenes the source does not contain, improve what is possible and list a few precise reporting questions instead of fabricating material.

Do not ask the user to choose a mode unless the choice would materially change what may be cut or rewritten.

## Recompose the piece

### 1. Establish the contract

Infer the purpose, reader, form, desired effect, factual constraints, and degree of authorial presence. Identify what the piece must make the reader understand, feel, question, or do.

### 2. Map the available material

Create a private inventory of:

- the central subject and the pressure, question, or change acting on it;
- the reader promise;
- supported claims and necessary qualifications;
- scenes, actions, examples, quotations, images, and concrete details;
- contradictions, turns, stakes, and unresolved questions;
- the strongest opening and ending material.

If every point seems equally important, the draft has not yet found its emphasis.

### 3. Find one spine

State privately: `This piece is about ___, under pressure from ___, so that the reader sees ___ differently.`

Choose one governing movement. Do not combine the most exciting fragments of several different stories. For pieces longer than a few paragraphs, read [structure-and-emphasis.md](references/structure-and-emphasis.md) and select an architecture that fits the available material.

### 4. Build an emphasis map

Rank the material:

- **A:** indispensable; give it position, space, specificity, or a deliberate pause;
- **B:** necessary support; connect and compress it;
- **C:** repeated, generic, or merely decorative; cut it.

Do not give every feature, argument, quotation, or paragraph equal weight. Emphasis comes from proportion and placement, not from adjectives, bold text, or repeated claims.

### 5. Choose the narrator

Decide who is speaking on the page and how that narrator relates to the reader and subject. Set the narrator's role, authority, distance, emotional temperature, formality, certainty, and humor. Read [voice-rhythm-and-tone.md](references/voice-rhythm-and-tone.md) whenever voice or tone is part of the request.

Do not apply a generic casual voice. A human voice can be restrained, technical, intimate, skeptical, delighted, severe, funny, or quiet.

### 6. Write from the new plan

Draft from the beat plan rather than patching the original sentence by sentence.

- Let each paragraph perform one move and create a reason for the next.
- Alternate scene, evidence, explanation, and reflection according to the form.
- Slow down for consequential material and move quickly through connective material.
- Prefer causal, temporal, or conceptual movement over generic transitions.
- Establish the piece's promise early without explaining the entire piece in advance.
- Design the ending to complete, complicate, echo, or honestly leave open that promise.

### 7. Shape the sound

Vary syntax because the thought changes, not to satisfy a rhythm formula. Use short sentences as brakes or impacts, long sentences for accumulation and flow, and fragments, repetition, triads, parentheses, or dashes only when they perform a clear job.

Read the draft aloud. Listen for an unbroken mid-length cadence, repeated paragraph shapes, fake punchlines, throat-clearing, and places where the prose sounds more certain or emotional than the evidence permits.

Reject canned reversal sentences such as "not X but Y," "it isn't X; it's Y," and "doesn't need X; needs Y." State the intended claim directly. Preserve an explicit contrast only when it corrects a real, consequential misunderstanding established by the material.

### 8. Apply sentence hygiene

Read [sentence-hygiene.md](references/sentence-hygiene.md). Remove generic AI habits without flattening deliberate style. Never ban a punctuation mark or construction merely because language models overuse it.

### 9. Verify integrity

Compare the rewrite with the source:

- Did it add a factual claim, scene, feeling, or observation?
- Did it lose a necessary claim or qualification?
- Does the emphasis reflect the piece's actual purpose?
- Does the opening create an honest promise?
- Does the ending deliver or deliberately complicate that promise?
- Could this narrator credibly know, notice, and say these things?
- Does any paragraph exist only because the original had one there?

Fix failures before returning the result.

## Return the work

- Return only the finished prose by default.
- Preserve or choose formatting that serves the form. Do not add headings, bold labels, or bullet lists merely to organize the answer.
- Add a short editor's note after the rewrite only when the source lacks material, a major structural choice changes interpretation, or the user asks for an explanation.
- When the user requests critique, lead with the structural diagnosis, then provide the rewrite.
- When editing a file, write the final prose to the file and summarize the material changes.
- Do not expose private inventories, rankings, or beat maps unless the user asks to see the editorial reasoning.
